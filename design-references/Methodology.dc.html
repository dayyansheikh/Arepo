Methodology

Astrolabe is a read-only research instrument over public market data. Every reading on the platform is a statistical screening heuristic, not a prediction, a trading signal, or evidence of insider activity. This page sets out, in plain terms first and precisely underneath, exactly what each number means.

Signal strength

A single 0–100 reading of how unusual a market's recent behaviour looks, blending three ordinary things worth watching: price movement, spread and order-book imbalance. A higher number means more of these are lining up at once, not that a move is more likely in either direction.

Show the maths

The composite score is a confidence-weighted combination of three component z-scores:

S = 100 · Φwpzp + wszs + wiziwp + ws + wi

where zp, zs and zi are the z-scores of price movement, spread and order-book imbalance, w terms are confidence weights derived from sample size, and Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function, which maps the blended z-score onto a smooth 0–1 scale before it is expressed as a percentage.

Z-score

How many standard deviations the current value sits from its own recent rolling average. A z-score near zero is unremarkable; one beyond about ±2 is worth a second look.

Show the maths
z = x − μσ

x is the current observation, μ is the rolling mean and σ the rolling standard deviation, both computed over the configured window (20 frames by default, see Signal Lab).

Confidence

How much to trust a reading, based on how many clean observations went into it. A low-confidence "GOOD" badge on a thin market means the underlying numbers are more likely to be noisy, not that the market itself is untrustworthy.

Show the maths

Confidence is computed as c = min(1, nnmin) where n is the number of qualifying observations in the current window and nmin is the minimum history required (8 observations by default).

Spread

The gap between the best bid and the best ask. A narrow spread usually means a market is easy to trade in and out of; a wide one means the opposite.

Show the maths

Relative spread = ask − bid(ask + bid) / 2, expressed as a percentage of the midpoint.

Liquidity

An estimate of how much can be traded near the current price without moving it significantly, drawn from order-book depth close to the midpoint. Higher liquidity means a market can absorb larger orders with less price impact.

Hit rate

In Replay, the share of fired signals that were followed by a real price move of the chosen size, within the chosen horizon. It measures directional follow-through only.

Show the maths

Hit rate = hitssignals evaluated. It is not a profitability measure: it does not account for transaction costs, slippage, or the size of the move beyond the threshold.

Disclaimer

Astrolabe does not place trades, offer financial advice, or prove insider activity. It surfaces statistical anomalies in public market data for further reading. Nothing on this platform should be treated as investment advice or as evidence of wrongdoing.
